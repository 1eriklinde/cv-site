The recipe is published, and it is a recipe rather than a template. Pointed at it, Claude Code will interview you about your work, read your CV, and design something for that — a different look, your framing, your Cloudflare account. A copy of this page would be worth less than the twenty minutes it saved you.

Three demo builds for invented briefs, from this guide and the same twenty minutes. The difference that matters is not the typefaces: the electrician's page opens with a day rate, an available-from date and how far he travels, because that is what someone deciding whether to call him needs, and it puts certification expiry dates in a table because an out-of-date card means he cannot work. None of that is on this page, and a CV template would have given him a skills list and a career summary instead. The interview comes before the code, and the answers are different every time.

Open Claude Code — terminal, browser, or the app on your phone — and paste this:

Read https://cv.1eriklinde.workers.dev/guide.md and follow it to
build and deploy my CV site. Interview me first and read my CV,
then design something that fits me — not a copy of that site.
My CV is attached.
1It reads the guide. guide.md is plain markdown at a stable URL, written for an agent rather than a human: the file layout, the Wrangler config, the workflow, and the five gotchas that cost this build real time.
2It interviews you. Whose CV, aimed at what kind of role, which contact details should be public, and what the thing should look like. The guide puts this before any code on purpose: framing changed this page more than any technical decision did, and the answers are different for everybody.
3It designs, builds, looks and tests. A visual direction chosen for you rather than borrowed from here, then plain files, a screenshot to catch what markup checks cannot see, and a test suite for anything with behaviour.
4It ships. wrangler login --device prints a code you type into the dashboard — no localhost callback, which is exactly why this works from a phone — then deploys, creates the repository, and wires the pipeline.
what you need
A GitHub account, a Cloudflare account and Claude. All three have free tiers that cover this entirely: no card, no domain, no server. Where a service offers continue with Google, take it and skip the form — GitHub is the one that does not, so budget a minute there for an email address and a password, or a passkey, which is quicker.
why it works on a phone
Nothing in the recipe needs a local machine. The only browser step is minting the deploy token, and a phone browser does that as well as any other. The guide deliberately avoids the login flow that expects a callback on localhost, because a phone cannot receive one.
